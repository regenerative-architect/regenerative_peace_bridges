# Restoration Economics

Open **Restoration Economics — lifecycle appraisal** above the planner tabs. The module creates an explicit, private appraisal for each saved plan. Outputs are deterministic simulations based on assumptions, not forecasts, verified causal effects or recommendations to invest. Earlier plans start with no appraisal.

## Boundary, prices and timing
Choose one currency/unit, a constant-price year, a horizon from 1 to 50 years, and real discount rates for a base case and lower/upper sensitivity cases. Explain the boundary, counterfactual, sources, rate rationale and exclusions. The supported rate range is 0–100%; these software bounds and example rates are not normative recommendations. Nominal inflation, foreign-exchange conversion, taxes, financing costs, residual asset values and cross-border transfers are not inferred. Add explicit lines with justified scope where appropriate. Avoid mixing nominal prices and real discount rates.

Year 0 is undiscounted. Later occurrences are discounted at year-end: `PV = value / (1 + real rate)^year`. First/last year and repeat interval determine occurrences; a one-off has equal first/last years. Real annual change (−50% to +50%) scales each occurrence by `(1 + change)^(year − first year)`. This can represent changing maintenance requirements or real unit costs, not an automatic inflation adjustment. The horizon truncates the schedule; account explicitly for later obligations and residual values rather than assuming they vanish.

## Lifecycle budgets and opportunity costs
Line types cover capital, maintenance, operating, replacement, decommissioning, opportunity cost, avoided loss, direct benefit and funding. Enter low/base/high amounts per occurrence, group bearing/receiving the flow, timing and assumptions. Base amounts must lie in the declared ranges. An included cost is assumed incremental to the counterfactual. A recurring maintenance projection is an assumed schedule, not a reliability or failure forecast.

Cash lifecycle costs exclude opportunity costs and funding receipts. Economic costs include cash costs plus separately justified opportunity costs. Opportunity cost is foregone **net** alternative value: do not count the same labour, land, inputs or revenue already captured in cash costs or net benefits. Existing planner budget entries remain independent and are not silently copied, updated or counted again; reconcile them manually if using the two views. A positive net present value does not ensure affordable cash flow; the engine does not model borrowing, liquidity or financing constraints.

## Avoided losses and ecosystem services
An avoided-loss line specifies loss without intervention and residual loss with intervention per occurrence. Its base value is their difference. Enter low/high values for the difference, with rationale. If residual loss is larger, the result can be negative. These are conditional monetary loss scenarios, not estimates of event probabilities or proof that an intervention prevents damage. Explain whether inputs are event-specific or already expected annual losses. Never multiply by a probability again if it is already embedded in the estimate; the engine applies none.

Every monetized direct benefit or avoided loss requires a **shared benefit-pathway key**. Included benefits with the same key and overlapping occurrence years block appraisal. Alternative valuations may be retained as excluded lines. Keys are normalized for case and surrounding whitespace. They must describe common causal pathways/endpoints and populations consistently; renaming a key is not a legitimate way to resolve duplication. Non-overlapping years are allowed, but users must still examine stocks versus flows and cumulative valuations.

Intermediate ecosystem services cannot be included as monetized benefits. For example, do not add a water-retention service valuation to avoided crop-loss benefits if the former produces the latter; use a final endpoint or retain the intermediate service as non-monetized context. Similarly, review replacement-cost estimates versus avoided damages, gross production versus net income, transfers, overlap between households, and capitalized asset values versus annual services. The app checks declared keys and valuation levels; it cannot infer undeclared causal overlap or certify a valuation. Document how additional benefit endpoints are distinct before including them.

## Local retention and distribution
Cash spending can allocate a percentage to named broad local recipient groups. The engine reports discounted direct local spending retained and its share of cash spending. This is a **gross direct-spending proxy**, not net local wealth, value added, a multiplier or an estimate of household welfare. Purchased inputs and subsequent leakage are not automatically estimated. Funding and local receipts remain transfer/distribution views; neither is added to social benefits or group net value.

For each stated bearer/beneficiary group, the report shows cost PV, benefit PV and net PV, with funding and gross local receipts in separate columns/lists. Use disjoint allocation lines when splitting costs or benefits between groups, taking care not to duplicate the same amount. Exact group labels determine grouping. Totals do not account for group size, vulnerability, within-group inequalities, fiscal incidence or consent. Use the community inclusion and stewardship tools to review distributional concerns; no automatic legitimacy or fairness score is generated.

## Sensitivity and non-monetized impacts
The report compares base assumptions with lower and upper discount rates and two amount cases: higher costs/lower benefits, and lower costs/higher benefits. Timing and growth assumptions remain fixed in these cases; edit them or fork the plan for additional comparisons. These cases are not confidence intervals or worst/best bounds. Net value may be non-monotonic in discount rates where flows change sign over time, and jointly chosen amount extremes may not be feasible. No probability distributions or Monte Carlo simulation are used.

Benefit–cost ratios are undefined when economic costs are zero, and retention shares are undefined when cash costs are zero. Negative avoided benefits remain visible. Undiscounted results are still constant-price amounts, not nominal future-currency totals.

Record ecological, cultural and social impacts separately with direction, affected groups, an indicator or qualitative review method, evidence, uncertainty and implications. Unpriced values are not zero: these records remain visible alongside monetary results and in JSON exports, without being summed into net value. Examples include cultural access, species observations, rights, loss of place, intergenerational effects and irreversible harms. Monetary results must not silently override these considerations.

## Storage, sharing and verification
Appraisals are local plan data, searchable through Search Everything and included in private backups, saved versions, fork design comparisons and offline/standalone builds. Forks preserve assumptions for review rather than treating them as locally validated. Public briefs omit appraisal data. Explicit JSON reports include the full saved model, deterministic calculations, limitations and non-monetized impacts through the existing safety gate. Reports are not import files; restore through plan backups. Use broad group labels and avoid identifying sensitive people or locations.

Limits: 200 monetary lines, 100 non-monetized impacts, 50 years, finite amounts up to one trillion per occurrence and bounded narrative fields. The existing combined fork-baseline size limit still applies. Changing the horizon refuses out-of-range saved lines; revise or remove them first. Settings, line edits and non-monetized entries save separately; unsaved drafts do not enter calculations.

The optional empty-appraisal example uses invented units, costs, discount rates, crop-loss values and local-retention shares solely to demonstrate the method. None are empirical findings or recommended parameters.

Run `node tests/economics-test.mjs`, `node tests/check-data.mjs`, and `node tests/browser-test.mjs --only Economics` (Playwright required). Tests include hand-calculated present values, year-zero and recurring costs, real growth, zero discounting, opportunity costs, transfer exclusion, negative avoided losses, local retention, group totals, duplicate pathways, non-monetized records and malformed inputs.
