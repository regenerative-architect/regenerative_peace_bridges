Yes. Looking at V2 in detail and comparing it to the themes you were pursuing in the earlier version, I think the differences fall into three groups:

Major capabilities added
Capabilities deliberately removed or de-emphasized
Major opportunities still missing

The most interesting finding is that V2 dramatically improved institutional robustness while simultaneously reducing systemic intelligence and movement-building capacity.

1. Major additions in V2

These are substantial upgrades rather than cosmetic changes.

Evidence Architecture

New:

Evidence grading model (A/B/C/How-To/Context)
Explicit source limitations
Link verification dates
Source provenance tracking
Citation-copying workflow
Evidence graph ("Evidence Web")
Document-to-bridge traceability

This is one of the biggest maturity upgrades.

V1 was closer to:

"Here are promising ideas."

V2 says:

"Here is exactly why this idea is here, what evidence supports it, and what the limitations are."






Consent Infrastructure

New:

FPIC integration
Consent logging
Withdrawal mechanisms
"No" pauses project progression
Consent script
Community meeting kit
Complaint pathway design

This is unusually strong.

Most humanitarian systems mention consent.

This architecture operationalizes it.






Conflict Sensitivity Layer

New:

Do No Harm integration
Connector/divider analysis
Anti-coercion covenant
Armed actor exclusion
Conflict risk framing
Mine action integration
Resource-conflict assessment

This is a major improvement over V1.







Practical Deployment Tools

New:

Meeting kit

Stakeholder identification tools

The project evolved from:

Knowledge archive

Operational planning environment






Offline Sovereignty

This is aligned with your broader archive philosophy.







Translation Governance

2. Things from V1 that appear reduced

These are not necessarily bad removals.

But they do change the nature of the project.

Planetary Narrative

V1 emphasized:

planetary healing
civilization repair
regenerative futures
global peace systems
humanity-scale restoration

V2 emphasizes:

projects
interventions
safeguards
implementation

Result:

Vision ↓

Credibility ↑

Systems-of-Systems Thinking

V1 was closer to:

ecosystem interactions
civilization resilience
multi-order effects
systems dynamics

Imagination

V1 invited:

"What else might become possible?"

V2 asks:

"How do we safely implement what is already documented?"

Much safer.

Potentially less revolutionary.





3. What is still missing

These are the largest gaps I see today.

Missing: Network Effects Layer

The biggest absence.

You have:

projects
bridges
evidence

But not:

bridge interactions
portfolio effects
system emergence

Example:

What happens if a community simultaneously implements:

FMNR
seed systems
school feeding
psychosocial support

The effects compound.

The tool currently treats them independently.



Missing: Dependency Mapping

You talk about readiness.

You do not yet model dependencies.

Example:

Safe land release → seed systems → food production → school feeding → peace outcomes

An explicit dependency graph would be powerful.



Missing: Impact Pathway Modeling

Current:

Bridge → implementation

Missing:

Bridge → Outputs → Outcomes → Second-order effects → Third-order effects

This is where funders increasingly think.



Missing: Peace Metrics Framework

You mention Positive Peace.

But it isn't deeply operationalized.

Still missing:

trust metrics
cooperation metrics
social cohesion metrics
grievance metrics
local governance metrics
relationship metrics
Missing: Community Asset Mapping

Planner identifies risks.

It does not deeply identify strengths.

For example:

local leaders
existing institutions
existing projects
traditional knowledge
cultural assets

Current framework tends toward deficit mapping.

A strengths inventory would balance it.



Missing: Knowledge Commons Architecture

This is the biggest omission relative to your broader archive vision.

Current model:

Reader consumes library.

Missing:

Community contributes knowledge.

Potential future additions:

local lessons learned
community observations
local indicators
adaptation records
bridge modifications

Right now the flow is mostly one-way.

Missing: Replication Intelligence

The bridges explain:

"What worked."

Missing:

"How many times has something like this succeeded?"

"Under what conditions?"

"When has it failed?"

This would be extremely valuable.

Missing: Failure Library

Interestingly none of the evidence system focuses explicitly on failure.

Huge opportunity.

Imagine:

Successes
Mixed Results
Failures
Why

That would make the archive unusually honest.

Missing: Governance Templates

You discuss governance.

You don't yet provide:

charter examples
committee structures
stewardship agreements
conflict resolution templates

Practical governance tools would strengthen implementation.

Missing: Scenario Exploration

One of the largest future opportunities.

Example:

"If rainfall drops by 30%"

"What if funding ends after 18 months?"

"What if local leadership changes?"

"What if population doubles due to displacement?"

Currently static.

Could become scenario-driven.

Missing: Restoration Economics

Current coverage:

budget checks

Missing:

ROI
avoided damage
ecosystem service valuation
maintenance economics
community wealth retention

Funders love this layer.

Missing: Legitimacy Tracking

A unique future feature.

Track:

community consent level
stakeholder inclusion
grievance response rate
participation quality

Essentially:

"social license to operate"

Missing: Movement Infrastructure

This is perhaps the largest philosophical gap.

V2 builds projects.

It does not yet build movements.

Missing:

shared learning
federated communities
bridge exchanges
practitioner networks
local adaptation communities

Right now users are planners.

Not collaborators.

Missing: Planetary Restoration Archive Integration

This is the one I expected most.

The platform references the archive, but doesn't yet fully leverage it.

Potential future integration:

interconnected archives
restoration cases
peace cases
ecological cases
adaptation cases
governance cases

The current bridge library feels somewhat standalone.

New opportunities now unlocked by V2

This is the exciting part.

Because V2 has become evidence-based and operational, entirely new classes of features are now possible:

Portfolio Builder

Combine multiple bridges into programs.

Readiness Scoring

Not gamified success scores. Readiness diagnostics.

System Simulation

Visualize interactions between bridges.

Regional Adaptation Profiles

Where specific bridges tend to fit.

Community-Led Research Layer

Local observations feeding future evidence.

Failure Learning Repository

Documenting what didn't work.

Peace & Restoration Dashboard

Operational indicators linked to bridges.

Regenerative Transition Pathways

Sequences of bridges over 5-10 years.

My Bottom-Line Assessment

If V1 was approximately:

Visionary Restoration Framework

and V2 became:

Evidence-Based Peace Restoration Operating System

then the largest thing still missing is:

A systems layer that explains how multiple safe, evidence-based interventions combine into long-term societal transformation.

You've largely solved the question:

"How do we implement regenerative peace ideas responsibly?"

You have not yet fully solved:

"How do many successful local bridges become planetary-scale restoration?"

That, in my opinion, is where the next major leap lies.
