# Regenerative Governance — version 3.6.0

Find **Regenerative Governance** in Solution bridges, or select it in the **Bridge category** filter. This is a dedicated bridge for deciding together, allocating shared resources, maintaining accountability and caring for projects over time. It can support work on water, food, restoration and other shared concerns.

The bridge starts at **Conceptual / Proposed**. A meeting, council or published budget does not automatically demonstrate consent, legitimate authority, improved governance or peace. The eight-step workflow is a proposal for local adaptation, not a tested combined intervention.

## Four parts of the bridge

| Part | What the community agrees | Failure to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Participatory budgeting | A real resource envelope, eligible proposals, assessment criteria, a decision method and maintenance costs | Consultation without spending authority; capture of allocations; hidden or unexplained costs |
| Community assemblies | Accessible information and participation routes, representation, quorum, deliberation and feedback | Exclusion, token attendance, decisions made in advance, coercion or minority concerns overridden |
| Stewardship councils | A bounded mandate, responsibilities, terms, rotation, recall, succession and review | Permanent unaccountable leadership, unclear responsibilities, unpaid burdens or competing institutions |
| Transparent decision-making | Options, reasons, responsible roles, declared interests, recusals, review dates and a safe appeal route | Opaque choices, conflicts of interest, unexplained rejection or exposure of complainants |

Start with one reversible maintenance decision using a small, disclosed resource envelope. Review who could influence it, who could not participate and what happened after the decision before expanding the mandate. Participation must be voluntary; this bridge does not establish legal authority or alter tenure rights.

## Use the existing planner

Choose **Plan this bridge**:

- **Timeline:** “Add this bridge’s steps” brings in the first step and eight pilot steps. Give milestones locally agreed dates, including budget reporting, council review and handover.
- **Budget:** record planned lines, actual changes and maintenance needs using the existing budget fields and notes. A budget entry is not a vote, an authorization or a payment instruction.
- **Risks:** “Add this bridge’s risks” adds seven governance failure modes. Assess likelihood, impact, mitigation and responsible collective roles with participants.
- **Consent log:** record group conditions and concerns, including refusal or withdrawal. Attendance or a majority vote must not be substituted for consent.
- **Measures:** select the seven bridge-specific measures as a monitoring checklist. Use existing Impact indicators for participation, women/youth leadership, trust, resolved disputes and connector institutions. Define a baseline, denominator, method and reporting period before comparing values. Additional budget/decision measures can be documented in notes or community accounts; they are not new automatic numerical charts.
- **Community knowledge:** capture what participants observed, lessons and unexpected outcomes. Three invented governance prompts support discussion; they are not evidence records.
- **Evidence Web:** the source diagram links this bridge to its guidance and accountability sources. System relationships can connect governance, leadership, land rights, restoration and outcomes as explicit hypotheses.
- **Solutions commons:** requests or offers can be associated with this bridge, for example mediation support, monitoring, knowledge or training. The commons remains non-financial; participatory budgeting is a governance practice, not a marketplace payment feature.

Lifecycle reviews remain explicit and independent of these activities. Data uses the existing plan schema, history, backups and offline storage; older plans do not need migration.

## A short mandate and decision-record template

Use this as a discussion aid, then record only appropriate non-identifying information in your plan:

1. **Purpose and limits:** Which decision is open? What is outside the group's authority? Who can pause the process?
2. **Participation:** Which affected groups have a route to contribute? What access, language, timing or care barriers must be addressed?
3. **Resources:** What amount or resource is actually available? What maintenance obligations continue afterwards?
4. **Decision rule:** How are options assessed, disagreement recorded and conflicts of interest handled?
5. **Stewardship:** Which collective roles implement and check the decision? When do their terms and responsibilities end or transfer?
6. **Public account:** What options were considered, what was chosen, why, and how will rejected proposals receive a response?
7. **Review and challenge:** When will outcomes be reviewed? How can people complain or appeal safely? Which information must remain confidential?

Do not infer trust from a low complaint count. Ask whether people know the route, can use it safely and receive meaningful responses. Do not infer influence from representation alone; ask whose input changed the decision. Publish aggregate decision and spending information while protecting confidential complaints.

## Sources and limits

The added [OECD Guidelines for Citizen Participation Processes (2022)](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2022/09/oecd-guidelines-for-citizen-participation-processes_63b34541.html) is a guidance source, with no causal evidence grade. Its official page was checked on 24 October 2026. It addresses participation design, budgeting, meetings, deliberation, feedback and evaluation.

The bridge also links to the existing library's UN Decade restoration principles, Core Humanitarian Standard, Do No Harm, FAO tenure guidelines and *Time to Listen*. Their existing source check dates are retained. Together these inform a proposed workflow; they do not establish a universal council structure or prove that the combined bridge prevents project failure.

The glossary defines participatory budgeting, community assemblies, stewardship councils and decision records. Bridge content is available through the existing reviewed-content translation mechanism; new category controls currently use marked English. The no-JavaScript version includes pilot steps and measures, and the standalone HTML includes the full governance bridge.
