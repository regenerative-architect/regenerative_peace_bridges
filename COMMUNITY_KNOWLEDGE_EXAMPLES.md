# Community knowledge examples — illustrative prompts only

These 72 invented scenarios are discussion prompts, not evidence about actual communities. Do not enter them as observed facts. Capture your own supported local account with context, source, limitations, review and permission.

## Seed sovereignty & nurseries

**Community observations**

> A seed-bank group recorded separate balances and losses for each variety.

Discussion prompt: Which records, dates and verification support this account, and what remains missing?

**Lessons learned**

> Tracking nursery survival separately from germination changed the next water-supply plan.

Discussion prompt: What denominators and observation dates were used, and what alternative explanations remain?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> A successful seed distribution reduced demand for a locally valued variety.

Discussion prompt: Who chose the varieties, whose preferences were overlooked and how can diversity be protected?

## Shared watersheds

**Community observations**

> Upstream and downstream groups continued their maintenance review through a dry season.

Discussion prompt: What period and attendance support this account, whose concerns remained unresolved, and did water access change?

**Lessons learned**

> Discussing downstream costs before choosing erosion works changed the pilot design.

Discussion prompt: Whose input changed the decision, and which impacts still need monitoring?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> A successful water-retention trial raised concerns about downstream availability.

Discussion prompt: What measurements and seasonal conditions support the concern, and what independent review is needed?

## Shared seed systems

**Community observations**

> Seed stewards from two communities compared germination results using an agreed method.

Discussion prompt: Were samples and conditions comparable, and were failed trials included?

**Lessons learned**

> Agreeing crop-failure provisions reduced uncertainty about seed replenishment obligations.

Discussion prompt: Who found the provisions fair, who disagreed, and what evidence supports the change?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Interest in an exchanged variety reduced demand for a locally valued seed.

Discussion prompt: Which preferences changed, and how can local diversity and farmer choice be protected?

## Cross-border restoration

**Community observations**

> Partner groups completed a joint review of their separate restoration pilots.

Discussion prompt: What commitments were reviewed, which gaps remained, and could participants safely disagree?

**Lessons learned**

> Parallel local pilots allowed ecological learning while travel arrangements remained unresolved.

Discussion prompt: Which learning could be shared, and what limitations did separate implementation introduce?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Different funding cycles left one partner unable to maintain its pilot.

Discussion prompt: How were responsibilities renegotiated without blame or unequal obligations?

## Joint monitoring

**Community observations**

> Two monitoring teams found different soil-cover estimates and documented the discrepancy.

Discussion prompt: What sampling effort, method and conditions could explain the difference?

**Lessons learned**

> Keeping disputed interpretations in the report helped partners continue the review.

Discussion prompt: Who felt heard, who did not, and did the review affect any decision?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Publishing comparisons was interpreted as ranking one community against another.

Discussion prompt: What feedback identified this effect, and what presentation would preserve learning without unfair comparison?

## Offline education hubs

**Community observations**

> Learners completed the selected lessons during an Internet outage.

Discussion prompt: Which lessons, period and failed attempts were recorded, and who could not access a device?

**Lessons learned**

> A rotating device schedule made supported learning sessions easier to attend.

Discussion prompt: Whose access improved, whose declined, and what did facilitators observe beyond attendance?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Keeping the hub open longer increased unpaid work for educators.

Discussion prompt: What workload and compensation arrangements need review?

## Community archives

**Community observations**

> The stewardship group restored its trial collection from a separate backup.

Discussion prompt: Was every item readable, and were permissions and catalogue context also recovered?

**Lessons learned**

> Reviewing permissions item by item clarified which accounts should stay private.

Discussion prompt: Who authorized each decision, and how can knowledge holders revise it?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> A shared historical account prompted disagreement between generations.

Discussion prompt: How can differing accounts be preserved without deciding which group owns the only valid history?

## Resilience information portals

## Regenerative Governance

**Community observations**

> Assembly participants could explain why one maintenance proposal was selected over another.

Discussion prompt: Who was asked, who was absent, and could people challenge the reasons without retaliation?

**Lessons learned**

> Separating spending approval from expenditure checks helped the council identify an unexplained cost.

Discussion prompt: What changed after review, and what alternative explanations or conflicts of interest remain?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Publishing meeting dates increased attendance but also increased unpaid care burdens for some participants.

Discussion prompt: Which groups faced new burdens, and what timing, access or compensation arrangements did they propose?

## Natural regeneration

**Community observations**

> Households reported protecting naturally regenerated trees through two dry seasons.

Discussion prompt: Which cohort, how were surviving trees checked, and were all land users represented?

**Lessons learned**

> Discussing tree rights before pruning helped clarify shared expectations.

Discussion prompt: What was agreed about use and ownership, and which disagreements remain unresolved?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Tree ownership became contentious after crop prices rose.

Discussion prompt: Whose claims changed, what alternative explanations exist, and what voluntary review is needed?

## Solar water

**Community observations**

> Water users reported shorter waits after agreeing a shared collection schedule.

Discussion prompt: What periods were compared, how were waits estimated, and who still faced delays?

**Lessons learned**

> Publishing maintenance costs helped residents discuss repair contributions.

Discussion prompt: Who could read or hear the information, and did households unable to pay feel pressured?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> The new pump increased demand faster than the local maintenance group expected.

Discussion prompt: What indicates rising demand, and what limits or maintenance changes need local review?

## Farmer–herder cooperation

**Community observations**

> Grazing corridor agreement remained stable for 18 months.

Discussion prompt: How was stability observed across seasons, whose accounts differ, and what counts as a breach?

**Lessons learned**

> Committee elections reduced conflict.

Discussion prompt: What changed after the elections, what else changed, and do all groups agree? Describe association rather than assuming causation.

**Unexpected outcomes**

> A new water point shifted herds toward fields outside the agreed corridor.

Discussion prompt: Which routes changed, who bore the costs, and how could the agreement adapt without identifying households?

## Healing landscapes

**Community observations**

> Participants described the community garden as a place where they felt comfortable meeting.

Discussion prompt: Was feedback voluntary and anonymous, and did anyone find the space uncomfortable or inaccessible?

> Offering quiet and group activities let people choose how to participate.

Discussion prompt: What preferences were expressed, and which access or support needs remain unmet?

**Unexpected outcomes**

Discussion prompt: Which scheduling options did residents suggest without naming individuals or recounting trauma?

## Zaï pits

**Community observations**

> Farmers observed that planting pits retained moisture longer than nearby untreated ground.

Discussion prompt: How and when was moisture observed, and were soil and rainfall conditions comparable?

**Lessons learned**

> Working in small groups made the initial digging effort easier to organize.

Discussion prompt: Who contributed labour, who could not, and was participation voluntary and fairly recognized?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Improved plots attracted grazing that damaged some emerging plants.

Discussion prompt: What observations support this, and which crop and livestock users should discuss adaptations?

## Safe land release

**Community observations**

> Residents reported greater confidence using land after formal release and community information sessions.

Discussion prompt: Distinguish perceived confidence from official clearance evidence; never infer safety from informal observations.

**Lessons learned**

> Explaining the formal release process helped residents ask questions about permitted land use.

Discussion prompt: Which questions remained, and how were people directed to qualified mine-action authorities?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Renewed access revealed overlapping land-use claims that had not been discussed before release.

Discussion prompt: What rights and consent processes are needed without publishing locations or naming claimants?

## Wetland restoration

**Community observations**

> Fishers reported seeing more young fish during the agreed monitoring season.

Discussion prompt: What survey effort, water conditions and species-identification limits affect this account?

**Lessons learned**

> Including fishers and grazing users in water discussions made trade-offs more visible.

Discussion prompt: Whose priorities were heard, and which water-sharing disagreements remain?

**Unexpected outcomes**

> Restored wet areas changed access routes used by some residents.

Discussion prompt: Who reported access difficulties, and what adaptations would protect both habitats and livelihoods?

## Safer cooking

**Community observations**

> Participants reported spending less time collecting fuel after trying the new cooking option.

Discussion prompt: What time estimates and season were compared, and did all household members share the benefit?

**Lessons learned**

> Cooking familiar meals during demonstrations helped households judge whether the option suited them.

Discussion prompt: Which preferences and costs mattered, and could households decline without losing support?

**Unexpected outcomes**
