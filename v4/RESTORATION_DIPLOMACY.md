# Restoration Diplomacy — version 3.8.0

Choose Restoration Diplomacy in the Solution bridges category filter. Four independent pathways connect shared watersheds, shared seed systems, cross-border restoration and joint monitoring to practical cooperation. Each includes prerequisites, six pilot steps, four risks, four safeguards and five measures, with links to relevant guidance.

## Learning rather than assuming peace

Ecological collaboration is a possible route to peacebuilding, not proof of it. Record ecological outcomes separately from participation, institutional response, disputes and voluntary trust accounts. A meeting, signed agreement or planted area is not itself evidence of reduced tension. Consider unequal power, exclusion, seasonal effects and other explanations. All four bridges begin at Conceptual / Proposed.

Use planner timelines and risks to seed the pilot. Budget facilitation, interpretation, monitoring, maintenance and review as well as materials. Record consent conditions and pause criteria. Use existing Impact indicators for soil cover, water retention, biodiversity, trees surviving, participation, leadership, trust, resolved disputes, shared projects and connector institutions as appropriate. Bridge-specific process measures can be recorded in custom plan measures and contextual observations; the fourteen structured impact indicators are unchanged.

Community knowledge includes twelve new fictional prompts, one observation, lesson and unexpected outcome for each pathway. They are not saved findings. In Evidence web → System relationships, select the Restoration Diplomacy example to explore shared ecological work → joint monitoring and review → trust → sustained cooperation. The map also includes feedback and the possibility that unequal benefits weaken trust. All links are hypotheses, local by default and editable; loading a template uses the existing replacement confirmation and history.

## Scope and safeguards

Community cooperation does not confer authority to negotiate treaties, determine boundaries or allocate resource rights. Cross-border activities require appropriate mandates and permissions. Start with non-sensitive knowledge exchange or parallel local pilots where movement is inappropriate. Before moving seed, obtain appropriate technical and regulatory review rather than assuming that exchange is permitted or ecologically suitable. These pathways do not arrange border crossings or publish sensitive locations.

Maintain voluntary participation, independent complaint routes, differing accounts and fair distribution of benefits. Respect knowledge-holder control. Share only approved summaries through existing knowledge and commons exchange; no automatic publishing or external messaging is added.

Three primary sources were opened on 25 October 2026: UNESCO’s 2024 transboundary cooperation overview, UNCCD’s Peace Forest Initiative and FAO’s 2014 Community seed banks facilitator guide. These are presented as guidance, not graded evidence of local peace effects. Source URLs and limitations are available on each bridge.
